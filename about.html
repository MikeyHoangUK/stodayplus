The student who followed the rules

I was the kid who did everything right. Extra classes. Revision packs. My mum spent money we didn't have on worksheets. I sat in classrooms where teachers stood at the front like gods, and we sat in silence, wasting time we'd never get back.

I studied hard. But I wasn't getting smarter. I was getting exhausted.

2

The crack

Then something broke. I realised this wasn't education — it was a system designed to keep students busy, not make them better. And I started asking why.

Why does everyone study the same way? Why does nobody question the grind? Why are we spending hours revising only to forget it all by exam day?

3

The system

I started paying attention. Testing methods. Looking at the science of how memory actually works — spaced repetition, active recall, the Forgetting Curve. I stopped copying notes and started testing myself. I stopped re-reading and started explaining things back to myself out loud.

I trained my brain. Not just for exams — for understanding.

4

The result

A-Levels I'm still proud of. An offer to study Pharmacy at one of the top schools in London. 20+ international Olympiad awards. Not from grinding harder — from thinking smarter.

Even when I couldn't afford to accept the offer, I knew one thing: I had built something powerful. And I wasn't going to keep it to myself.
